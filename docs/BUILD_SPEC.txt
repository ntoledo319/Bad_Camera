1. DELIVERABLE AND DEFINITION OF SUCCESS

Deliver one maintainable cross-platform native mobile codebase for iPhone and Android, with platform-appropriate behavior and a browser preview for convenient design review. The browser preview is a companion, not the deliverable in place of native applications.

The app must let someone:
1) Open a map or accessible list without an account.
2) Locate publicly documented camera/sensor infrastructure near a chosen place.
3) See approximate straight-line distance from their current or manually selected reference point, with meaningful accuracy and freshness information.
4) Understand what the hardware is, what the source actually establishes, and what remains unknown.
5) Photograph or import a photo, place the equipment on the map, identify it using a guided field guide, attach sources, and save a private observation.
6) Preserve the original evidence and an inspectable history while making privacy-redacted derivatives for sharing.
7) Export beautiful social cards, a readable evidence PDF, and a machine-readable evidence package.
8) Understand the legal basis and limits of lawful public observation, mapping, and publication through a substantive cited rights page.
9) Back up and restore their notebook without creating an account.

No login, guest-login screen, anonymous Firebase account, mandatory phone number, email collection, subscription gate, advertising ID, social OAuth, or required cloud account for app users. Developer signing/provider accounts are separate deployment concerns and must never become end-user login requirements.

The core map, field guide, records, redaction, exports, rights content, and backup must genuinely function. No fake scan animation, random confidence score, hard-coded "live" count, dummy save, fake share-success toast, or buttons leading to unfinished dead ends. An explicitly labeled demo dataset is permitted only in a separate demo mode.

9. SOURCE INGESTION, COVERAGE AND LICENSING

Primary starting point: OpenStreetMap surveillance/ALPR records. DeFlock's public project demonstrates the community mapping ecosystem, but is not an official complete Flock inventory and does not imply a supported unrestricted private API. Its mobile source is AGPL-3.0; build an original implementation. Do not copy its app code or assets into a differently licensed project without satisfying the license. OSM data licensing and app-source licensing are separate. [D1,D2,D3]

Use OSM stable IDs and source versions. Recognize man_made=surveillance with surveillance:type=ALPR/camera/gunshot_detector; retain raw tags. Normalize case/known synonyms carefully and record normalization rules. Treat highway=speed_camera and appropriate enforcement relations as a separate adapter. Unknown surveillance types must not default to ALPR. Avoid treating relation centroids or agency addresses as exact physical camera coordinates. [D3]

Implement a bounded ingestion CLI and a read-only region adapter. Baseline pipeline:
 - Fetch a chosen bounded region using an allowed OSM extract/Overpass endpoint.
 - Validate geometry, attribution, timestamps, content type, limits and stable IDs.
 - Normalize into installation/claim/source tables.
 - Write region.json (or compressed equivalent), manifest, attribution and reproducible ingestion log.
 - Preserve published observation dates separately from source timestamps.
 - Merge source updates without overwriting private records or deleting observations.
 - Missing on one refresh is not proof of physical removal.

Provide an actual working small-region retrieval path for initial testing; choose a modest area around Fairfield, CT as the default optional import, not fabricated local pins. Do not assert any individual local installation unless retrieved from a real source. Record what was fetched and when. If upstream is unavailable, allow user-provided GeoJSON import and make the live-source blocker visible. A demo dataset cannot silently fill gaps in real mode.

For development, bounded user-triggered requests to a policy-compliant public endpoint are acceptable. For production, generate/copy permitted regional extracts to an owner-configured static data host/CDN; don't have every phone download the U.S. from public Overpass. The app receives versioned manifests and only fetches selected regions. Backend is not required for the notebook. Public feeds cannot contain user private observations.

Upstream requests: one in flight per provider, timeouts, cancellation, exponential backoff with jitter, respect Retry-After, cache responses, request-size ceiling, safe JSON parsing and preserved stale data. Defaults: 30s network timeout, at most 3 retries; repeated failure returns control to the user. No stealth scraping or endpoint evasion. Validate the selected endpoint's current policy before use. [D7]

Map/data attribution must be visible in app and exports that use that data. Bundle the applicable ODbL notice and fulfill database-distribution obligations, including offering the relevant derived database under its required license where applicable. Do not assume an OSM-derived image makes unrelated private photographs publicly licensed. Preserve license boundaries; document any merged dataset decisions. [D2]

Basemap provider behind an interface. For small interactive development use, OSM standard raster tiles may be used only in compliance with current identification, caching and attribution rules. Never prefetch those tiles for offline packs. Production provider/offline provider is configurable; a provider needing payment/key is an explicit external dependency, not a fake free service. Camera records and basemap tiles have different rights and lifecycles. [D4]

Search defaults: bundled selected-place index, coordinate input, and local downloaded-region search. Optional remote geocoding on explicit submit, with provider disclosure. Do not implement public Nominatim autocomplete or assume a per-device throttle satisfies its aggregate policy. A production geocoder must be permitted for the actual load; default offline search remains useful. [D5]

EFF Atlas is useful for agency technology adoption and context. Its own page directs individual camera locations elsewhere. Represent it as agency-level evidence unless a particular source provides installation coordinates. Verify current dataset license before ingesting; initially link to relevant research rather than pretending agency centroids are camera locations. [D6]

10. DISTANCE ENGINE AND HONEST MEASUREMENT

Use WGS84 latitude/longitude inputs and calculate approximate geodesic/straight-line surface distance. Implement a pure tested function; Haversine with R=6,371,008.8m is sufficient for nearby consumer display, explicitly labeled approximate. Clamp floating-point terms to valid ranges. Handle longitude wrap, antimeridian and identical positions. Retain raw computed meters in records; round only in the UI.

Proposed UI rounding:
 - Under 100m: nearest 5m / 10ft, unless uncertainty warrants coarser display.
 - 100m–1km: nearest 10m / 50ft.
 - Longer: one decimal km/mi, avoiding meaningless precision.
 - If uncertainty exceeds distance: "Very close to the mapped location; exact distance uncertain." No 0.0m claim.

Timestamp each reference fix. Foreground active mode may use a watch; stop it when the relevant screen/session ends. At >30s age mark the fix old; at >2min label distance "from last location" and stop suggesting it is live. Poor horizontal accuracy >50m gets a visible approximate-location label. These are product thresholds, not guaranteed measurement physics. Manual reference points are never called "your current location."

Display both phone-reported accuracy and source-location uncertainty. Only if both uncertainties are explicitly known, optionally show an illustrative distance envelope using max(0,d-a-b) to d+a+b; label it a rough envelope, not a statistical confidence interval. Unknown source uncertainty stays unknown. Distance to a mapped coordinate is not distance to a camera's optical coverage boundary.

Do not calculate "you were recorded," "in view," "safe zone," or "camera range" from distance. Do not fabricate angle, focal length, power status or line of sight. Source-documented orientation can appear as a direction tick with its own provenance. Published technical ranges belong in a sourced catalog note, not as a live coverage circle.

14. LEGAL PAGE — READY COPY AND REQUIRED VALIDATION

Use this substantive draft as the U.S. overview. Rewrite only for factual correction, readability or localization; preserve qualifications and citations. This is legal information, not individualized advice. It is not a declaration that all uses or all surveillance deployments are lawful. Obtain a qualified legal review before marketing it as reviewed legal guidance.

TITLE: Why public camera mapping can be lawful
SUBTITLE: Public observation, truthful documentation and responsible sharing — with real boundaries.

LEAD:
Sightline is designed to help you observe equipment from places you may lawfully be, keep your own records, and read or share lawfully obtained public information. It does not access camera feeds or law-enforcement databases. In the United States, the First Amendment provides an important basis for public photography, information gathering and discussion of government activity. The details depend on location, conduct and the applicable law. [L1,L2]

PUBLIC OBSERVATION:
The ACLU explains that photographing things visible in public spaces is generally constitutionally protected. Public visibility is not permission to enter someone else's property: a camera visible from a sidewalk and a camera inside a restricted area present different access questions. Sightline is built around observation from lawful vantage points. Property rules, restricted facilities and valid safety restrictions still matter. [L1,L3]

RECORDING GOVERNMENT ACTIVITY:
In Fields v. City of Philadelphia (Third Circuit, 2017), the court recognized First Amendment protection for recording officers performing public duties. It also explained that this right is subject to limits, including reasonable time, place and manner restrictions and interference concerns. This is an important example of the legal framework; it is not a Supreme Court ruling about every surveillance map or automatic nationwide permission for every recording. [L2]

READING AND SHARING SOURCES:
The app supports public understanding by distinguishing a document's contents, an observation and an inference. Use sources you are entitled to access, credit licensed datasets and photographs appropriately, and describe uncertainty. A map pin does not establish that its owner acted illegally or recorded you. The right to discuss surveillance does not erase privacy, copyright or other applicable obligations. [D2; application design rationale]

WHAT THE APP DOES NOT AUTHORIZE:
Do not trespass, interfere with officers or traffic, damage or obstruct equipment, or access a system without authorization. Still photography and recording a private conversation are not the same legal issue; audio-recording rules vary. The core capture flow takes photographs and does not request microphone access. Do not use this tool to harass people or publish unsupported accusations. [L3; product restrictions]

PUBLIC RECORDS:
Public-records laws can help you learn which agency bought a system and what policies govern it. For Connecticut agencies, the relevant starting point is the Connecticut Freedom of Information Act, including section 1-210; federal FOIA is not the default law for a town police department. Exemptions, redactions and fees can apply. The request builder prepares a draft for you to review and send; it does not guarantee disclosure. [L4]

WHAT OUR RECORDS PROVE:
A record can preserve the photograph you supplied, the information you entered, the source version saved, and the app's distance calculation. A checksum helps compare file integrity. Neither a checksum nor a location pin establishes that a camera was active, captured you or shared your data. Device timestamps and locations are not independently verified. [application methodology]

This overview concerns the United States. Rules can differ by state and place and change over time. Read the linked sources and seek local legal advice for a specific dispute. Each article shows when its sources were checked. No reviewed guidance is provided here for other countries.
